# Start here — Open Resource Dividend (ORD)

**Two-minute explanation · Public research proposal, not a live currency**

## Why consider this?

An economy with increasingly capable AI could concentrate production around owners of compute, energy, infrastructure and materials. Universal Basic Compute is one response. ORD asks a different question:

**Can everyone hold a real, freely exchangeable economic entitlement, while being free to use AI heavily, hardly at all, or not at all?**

The goal is **open economic agency**, not forcing anyone to participate in AI and not giving one AI provider authority to issue everyone's money.

## The idea in one example

Frank likes a simpler life and barely uses AI. Bob wants additional AI compute for a project. Both receive a periodic allocation **if an independently funded distribution pool exists**. Frank can save his claims, exchange them with Bob for something he wants, or request their underlying gold, silver or copper under clearly specified redemption conditions. Bob can buy compute from competing sellers. Nobody is required to spend on AI, and nobody is required to make a trade.

The open research hypothesis is that physically backed, transferable entitlements could allow wider participation in the wealth of an AI-transformed economy without dependence on an AI company.

## Five things ORD would need

| Component | Intended property | Still unresolved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Physical backing | Precisely defined ownership/redemption claims on **verified** gold, silver or copper | Title, custody, grade, quantity, fees and legal enforcement |
| Universal dividend | Periodic transfer of **existing, funded claims** | Who contributes real assets or income at a meaningful scale? |
| Open exchange | Hold, gift, exchange, redeem, or buy goods/services including compute | Liquidity, market power, ordinary transaction costs |
| Independently verifiable accounting | Public append-only events for issuance, transfers and burns | Governance, validator capture, privacy, independent liability audits |
| Provider-neutral compute market | Sellers compete for holders' purchasing power | Energy/semiconductor bottlenecks, interoperability, provider concentration |

An immutable ledger cannot verify a vault contains what it claims. An allowance cannot be universal and indefinitely funded just because a smart contract promises to mint it. These are material economic and governance constraints, not implementation footnotes.

## What's already been tried?

- **Universal Basic Compute** — distributing access to AI compute (Sam Altman).
- **Windfall Trust** — research on a universal stake in large AI-generated wealth.
- **Alaska Permanent Fund** — annual resident payments linked to mineral-derived wealth.
- **Kinesis** — independently audited gold/silver-linked digital currencies with physical redemption requirements.
- **GoodDollar and Circles** — differing models of blockchain-based basic income.
- **Akash** — competing providers of computation.

These are **related precedents**, not ORD deployments. See [source links and comparison](PRIOR_ART.md). No exhaustive novelty search has been completed.

## The first question worth testing

> **What sustained source of legally owned physical resources can finance global distributions without issuing unbacked claims?**

We have documented candidate funding approaches in [FUNDING_MODELS.md](FUNDING_MODELS.md). A [tiny, dependency-free stock-flow example](examples/README.md) demonstrates accounting conservation **under assumed physical verification**, not real-world feasibility.

## How to contribute

Read the [white paper](WHITEPAPER.md), [open questions](OPEN_QUESTIONS.md), and [contribution guide](CONTRIBUTING.md). An objection with a worked counterexample is as useful as a supportive suggestion. Please identify stronger precedents, check underlying economic assumptions, or propose an independently auditable route to funded distribution.

**No token offering, custody, fundraising, investment return, universal payment or live network exists.** A proposed claim on metal is not backed unless the actual metal and enforceable legal rights exist.
