# Wholesale delivery evidence and the retail redemption gap

**ORD research note · 31 October 2026 · dated primary market rules, not ORD contractual terms**

One of ORD's essential claims is that Frank should be free to hold and redeem physical resources, rather than being forced to spend on AI or on any provider's closed credits. This raises a material difference between owning a *fractional legal interest* and actually collecting that fraction as deliverable metal.

## Illustrative magnitude check (not a cost quote)

The global copper [scale envelope](COMMODITY_SCALE.md) finds **28.512 grams per person per year**, under a *hypothetical* 1%-of-2025-world-mine-production contribution across 8.3 billion people. A standard LME Grade A 25-tonne delivery lot represents about **876,800** such annual 28.512-gram claims by mass alone. This **does not** mean the mine output was eligible Grade A copper, that a trust owns it, or that fractionally held LME warrants grant direct retail access.

Mathematical divisibility is not physical divisibility, delivery, legal priority or free market liquidity.

For independent metal redemption, a future asset/custody design must distinguish:

1. **Title:** Does the holder own an identifiable fraction of specific metal under applicable property law, or merely an unsecured right against a company?
2. **Warehouse access:** Can the holder direct release, assign title, aggregate with others, or appoint a delivery agent without the issuer's unilateral discretion?
3. **Lot conversion:** If small claims are backed by a large warehouse lot, who pays for assay, cutting, fabrication, packaging, shipping, taxes and liability while the lot is divided?
4. **Exit freedom:** Can the holder sell to any interested buyer, transfer to another custodian, or redeem through multiple competing routes? Is there a single chokepoint?
5. **Insolvency:** If an operator or warehouse fails, is the holder's property identifiable and separately held, or pooled with the custodian's creditors?
6. **Auditability:** Does a third-party auditor independently inspect physical inventory, legal title and **all** associated promises (including derivatives, bridges and off-ledger claims)?

### Worked exit-economics comparison (not a market price quote)

Use the **deliberately illustrative, static** reference prices already published in [three-metal inputs](THREE_METAL_COMPARISON.md): gold **US$4,000 per fine troy oz**, silver **US$75 per troy oz**, copper **US$14,000 per metric tonne**. The conversion for the gold row is **31.1034768 grams per troy ounce**. These are **not live bids**, product prices, future purchasing-power forecasts or a recommended acquisition. For comparison only, assume a hypothetical fully funded **US$100 equivalent per person per year** in *the relevant single metal*, accumulated without expenses, changes in price or dilution.

| Direct physical route | Minimum mass/quantity | Reference metal value at the hypothetical price | **Published identifiable charges** illustrated at that value | Minimum years to accumulate reference value at $100/year, *before* charges |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: |
| Kinesis gold | 100 g = ~3.215075 troy oz | **$12,860.30** | **~$157.87** = 0.45% of $12,860.30 + $100; **shipping/packing extra** | ~**129 years** |
| Kinesis silver | 200 troy oz | **$15,000.00** | **$167.50** = 0.45% of $15,000 + $100; **shipping/packing extra** | **150 years** |
| Paxos PAXG allocated bar | 430 fine troy oz | **$1,720,000.00** | **$2,150** at the first redemption band of 0.125% *only if that rate applies*; additional delivery and potentially applicable conversion costs **not included** | **17,200 years** |
| LME copper physical warrant (not retail) | 25 metric tonnes | **$350,000.00** | **No consumer delivery-cost quote established** | **3,500 years** (wholesale lot, not a retail withdrawal mechanism) |

**Transparent formulas:** Kinesis gold value = `100 / 31.1034768 * 4000`; Kinesis silver value = `200 * 75`; Kinesis minimum disclosed fee = `0.0045 * reference_value + 100`; Paxos reference bar value = `430 * 4000`; illustrative first-band charge = `0.00125 * reference_value`; wholesale copper = `25 * 14000`. Accumulation years = `reference_value / 100`, rounded **up** for whole years. This is simple arithmetic, not a return model. For Kinesis, the *fixed $100 administration* alone is equivalent to a full year's hypothetical $100 benchmark even before percentage fees, delivery, storage, tax, service eligibility or counterparty risk.

**Do not interpret the table as an economic impossibility proof.** Small holders could voluntarily sell claims, accumulate over time, elect competing authorised physical outlets, or consent to an aggregator receiving and dividing delivered lots. Whether those exits are **lawfully assignable, non-exclusive, affordable, insolvency-protected and available globally** is not established by the cited provider documents. Shared aggregation also introduces custody and consent risks: the aggregator cannot silently become an unaccountable new issuer.

### Implications for ORD's physical-ownership promise

An independent funding mechanism must solve **at least two different economic problems**: actual receipt of unencumbered metal rights into the distributable pool, and a practical **low-value exit** for an individual who does not use AI. A fully backed account at a wholesale custodian may fail the second requirement. The [LBMA account distinctions](https://www.lbma.org.uk/publications/the-otc-guide/precious-metal-accounts) also caution that an unallocated account is generally a **contractual claim on the clearer**, not specific allocated bars held for the account owner; swapping a tiny allocated claim for an unallocated claim changes the credit exposure and must not be presented as identical property protection.

A future design should disclose **per-metal and per-jurisdiction** minimums, fee schedules, legal title upon transfer, who legally holds undivided shares, optional aggregation arrangements, audit of total liabilities, and independent exit routes. No third-party service has been admitted as an ORD asset partner and no ORD recipients have received any resource claims.

## Not yet established

Customer-facing **gold and silver** redemption fees and minimums have now been incorporated from Kinesis and Paxos. A comparable date-verified **consumer copper redemption** route, actual country-specific delivery quotes, full applicable Paxos bar-delivery fee combination, and independently verified cross-custodian retail exit remain **unestablished**. Do not call LME wholesale lots retail minima, or fabricate logistics/insurance charges. The existing [first contribution issue #10](https://github.com/Christopher-Charman/open-resource-dividend/issues/10) remains open for independent data on charges, minimum retail orders, storage terms and legally enforceable fractional ownership.

### Hypothesis this evidence challenges

A claim might be economically tradeable on a secondary market yet so inconvenient to redeem physically that its practical value derives mostly from continuing third-party market bids. If that happens, the proposal's promise of *independent resource redemption* is weaker than advertised. ORD must demonstrate a credible non-issuer exit route, or narrow the claim and disclose its dependency.

**This note does not assert ORD physically owns any metal, that an external exchange has endorsed it, or that any retail redemption service is available.**
